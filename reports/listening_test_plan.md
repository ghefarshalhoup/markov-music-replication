# Listening test plan (RQ4)

Draft, 2026-11-17. Nothing has been run yet.

This test is the last comparison the brief asks for: "musical evaluation (listening tests)".

## 1. Question and expectations

**Question.** Do listeners hear a difference between the baseline and the MDP, and how do both compare with real melodies?

The expectations below are fixed before anyone listens. They follow from what the MDP changed: the ending and the beat accent, and nothing else.

| | Expectation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Endings:** MDP pieces are rated as ending more "finished" than baseline pieces | The MDP ends on a long C in most pieces; the baseline ends anywhere |
| H2 | **Liking:** MDP pieces are liked at least as much as baseline pieces | Everything except the ending is unchanged |
| H3 | **Human or computer:** MDP pieces are judged "human" more often than baseline pieces | A proper ending is a strong cue of a composed melody |
| H4 | **Real melodies:** real excerpts score highest on all three | The range and silence gaps remain in both models |

## 2. What listeners hear

There are three conditions, with 10 clips each, so 30 clips in all:

| Condition | Source | How the clips are chosen |
|---|---|---|
| Real | the 10 test songs | 8-bar excerpts ending at a phrase end (rule below); 10 drawn at random |
| Baseline | the n = 1 baseline | the first 10 of its 200 evaluation pieces (fixed seed) |
| MDP | the main version (matched strength, leash) | the first 10 of its 200 evaluation pieces (fixed seed) |

**Choosing clips fairly.**
- **No hand-picking.** The generated clips are simply the first ones from the fixed-seed runs.
- **Real endings.** Real excerpts on our fixed 8-bar grid usually stop mid-phrase: only 8 of the 74 end at a phrase end. That would be unfair to the real melodies on the endings question. So real clips are the 8 bars that end at the first bar line after a **phrase end**, meaning a note followed by at least 2 beats of silence. Silence before the first note is trimmed to under one beat, as in generated pieces.

**All clips sound the same way.**
- Same synthesizer and instrument (one piano sound).
- Same tempo: 90 beats per minute, so 8 bars last about 21 seconds.
- Same loudness mapping from levels to velocities.
- The same rule for note ends (each note held until the next note starts) for real and generated clips alike.
- Each clip ends with one second of silence and is normalised to the same overall volume.

So the only difference between conditions is the notes themselves.

## 3. Procedure

1. **Who listens.** 20–30 people, for example classmates, friends and family. No musical training needed.
2. **Before starting.** One question about musical experience (none / some / trained), and a consent line. Nothing else is recorded: the test is anonymous.
3. **What each person hears.** 15 clips, 5 from each condition, drawn at random from the 30. They play in random order and are labelled only "Clip 1–15". It takes about 10 minutes.
4. **The three questions** asked after each clip:
   - *How much do you like this melody?* (1 = not at all, 5 = very much)
   - *Does the ending sound finished?* (1 = cut off, 5 = clearly finished)
   - *Was this melody written by a person or generated by a computer?* (person / computer)
5. **Attention checks.** Two extra clips: one plays a single note repeated for 8 bars, the other a plain C major scale. A response sheet that rates the repeated note above 3 for liking is left out.
6. **Pilot first.** Two people try it before the real test, to check timing and clarity. Their answers are not counted.

## 4. Analysis (fixed in advance)

- **Per condition:**
  - the mean of each rating, with a 95% interval from resampling listeners (bootstrap over people, 10,000 draws);
  - the share of "person" answers.
- **MDP against baseline (H1–H3):**
  - each listener's mean for MDP clips minus their mean for baseline clips;
  - test: Wilcoxon signed-rank;
  - effect size: the median difference with its 95% interval.
- **Reporting.** Everything is reported, whatever the result. With about 25 listeners, small effects may not be detectable, so intervals matter more than p-values.
- **Optional extra.** Results split by musical experience, if each group has at least 8 people.

## 5. What needs building (after your go-ahead)

1. **Stimuli script.** It picks the clips by the rules above and writes MIDI files with the same settings.
2. **Audio.** The MIDI files are turned into MP3 with one piano sound. Options:
   - FluidSynth with a free piano SoundFont;
   - exporting from MuseScore in batch.
3. **The form.** Two options:
   - a Google Form with the audio linked (simple and familiar);
   - a small web page that plays the clips in random order and stores the answers (more control over randomisation).
4. **Analysis script.** It reads the answers and writes `results/listening/summary.md`.

## 6. Limits to state in the report

- The listeners are a convenience sample, not experts.
- The piano sound and held notes make every clip sound somewhat mechanical. This is the same for all conditions, but it may lower all ratings.
- The loudness accent (about 3 velocity steps) is probably too small to hear, so the test mainly measures the ending.
